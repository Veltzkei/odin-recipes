PULAO

Pulao simply means a dish where long grain rice is cooked in a highly seasoned, aromatic broth so that every single grain absorbs all the rich flavor. If you cannot find long grain rice (like Basmati), you can easily substitute it with any medium grain white rice or jasmine rice. Just ensure you rinse it well to keep the grains separate.

MUTTON PULAO

Out of all the varieties out there, we selected Mutton Pulao because nothing compares to the way tender, juicy mutton fuses with fragrant basmati rice to create the ultimate comfort food.

In this quick guide, we will look directly into two main highlighted things to get you through this successfully:

  • The basic requirements (the essential ingredients available to anyone)
  • The step-by-step execution process

We will follow this journey from the very first sizzle in the pot all the way until it hits your dining table, steaming hot and ready to eat!

The History of Mutton Pulao

Pulao has ancient roots tracing back to old Central Asian and Persian culinary traditions, where it was originally known as Pilaf.

Over centuries, as traders and kings traveled across borders, the dish arrived in South Asia. Here, local chefs adapted the cooking style by incorporating rich regional spices, tender goat meat or mutton and aged basmati rice. To learn more about the history of Pulao breakdown here in this link wikipedia's history of pilaf.

This fusion transformed the ancient recipe into the modern Mutton Pulao we love today a celebratory centerpiece cooked in royal courts and family kitchens alike. History of Pulao Revealed Spice Jin.

Now that we understand the rich heritage behind this legendary dish, let us dive into the core foundations. To prepare a traditional yet accessible version at home, ensure you gather these basic requirements before starting the cooking process:

  • Mutton ⇒ 500g, bone in for the best flavor
  • Basmati Rice ⇒ 2 cups, thoroughly rinsed and soaked
  • Onions ⇒ 2 large, thinly sliced
  • Ginger&Garlic Paste ⇒ 1 tablespoon
  • Whole Spices ⇒1 bay leaf, 1 cinnamon stick, 4 cloves, and 4 green cardamoms
  • Cooking Oil or Ghee ⇒ 3 tablespoons
  • Water ⇒ 4 cups, to cook the meat and rice (might add extra depending)
  • Salt ⇒To taste

Step by Step Execution Process⇒ Now that your kitchen counter is fully prepped with all the basic requirements, it is time to bring the flavors to life. We will guide you through each critical phase from the first crackle of whole spices to the final steam cook ensuring your basmati rice absorbs every bit of savory mutton richness. Follow these sequential steps carefully to achieve absolute perfection.

Step 1⇒ Browning the Aromatics
  1. Heat the cooking oil or ghee in a deep cooking pot.
  2. Add the thinly sliced onions and fry them steadily until they turn a deep, rich golden brown.
Step 2⇒ Searing the Meat and Spices
  1. Toss the whole spices (bay leaf, cinnamon stick, cloves, and green cardamoms) directly into the hot onion oil to release their essential oils.
  2. Add the ginger & garlic paste and the 500g of bone in mutton.
  3. Sear the meat on high heat until the raw pink color completely disappears and the outside turns brown.
Step 3 ⇒ Simmering the Mutton (Yakhni)
  1. Pour in water and add salt to taste.
  2. Bring the liquid to a boil, then turn the heat down low, cover the pot tightly, and let it simmer until the mutton is completely tender and breaks apart easily.
Step 4 ⇒ Incorporating the Rice
  1. Drain the pre-soaked basmati rice completely and gently stir it into the pot with the tender meat and broth.
  2. Cook on medium high heat until the rice absorbs most of the liquid and small steam holes form on the surface.
Step 5 ⇒ The "Dum" Steam Finish
  1. Seal the top of the pot tightly with a lid or foil to trap all remaining steam.
  2. Keep the heat on the absolute lowest setting for 15 minutes, allowing the rice to fluff up perfectly without sticking.

Your fragrant Mutton Pulao is now ready to be enjoyed! For the best culinary experience, serve it piping hot alongside a cool cucumber raita (yogurt dip), fresh sliced red onions, and a crisp green salad.
